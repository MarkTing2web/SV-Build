The New Build Advantage

The Window That Only Opens Once

A Singapore new build offers a single window for perfect security—before the walls close and conduit runs become remedial hacking. By engaging Securevision at the structural stage, we coordinate with your architect and M&E team to design security into the house, ensuring ideal coverage and concealed cabling at a fraction of the retrofit cost. Once walls are plastered and ceilings boarded, your security options are constrained by your finishes, not by engineering. Plan early to avoid the compromises of an afterthought.

🏗️

Before Plastering

All cable runs, conduit, and back-boxes are installed while walls are open. Camera positions are chosen for coverage — not for wherever a surface-mount bracket will fit. This is the difference between a system that works perfectly and one that almost works.

📐

Coordinated With Your M&E

Securevision works directly with your main contractor, electrical sub-contractor, and architect to integrate security into the M&E schedule. Our wiring schedules and cable specifications are provided in the format your M&E team expects — no coordination friction, no rework.

💰

A Fraction of the Retrofit Cost

Pre-wiring a 4-bedroom Singapore landed home during construction typically costs 60–70% less than the same installation after completion. The saving comes entirely from labour — no hacking, no patching, no repainting. The hardware cost is identical.

Construction Stage Guide

When We Do What — and Why It Matters

Security planning on a new build follows the construction programme. Miss a stage and you miss the opportunity it creates.

1

Structural Stage

Confirm camera positions, gate motor locations, and alarm panel location. Submit cable schedule to M&E. Issue conduit spec for electrical sub-contractor to install.

2

First Fix (M&E Rough-In)

Supervise conduit installation, install back-boxes and draw-wires, confirm NVR and panel board positions, coordinate network switch locations with data contractor.

3

Second Fix (Post-Plastering)

Pull cables, terminate to back-boxes, install camera bodies and reader frames. All visible components installed at this stage.

4

Commissioning (Pre-Handover)

Install final equipment, configure all systems, connect to network, test every camera, sensor, and gate. Train the homeowner. Produce as-built drawings.

We can join a project at any stage — but the earlier we are involved, the better the outcome and the lower the final cost. The ideal entry point is at or before the structural stage.

Systems We Pre-Wire

What Goes Into a New Build Security System

Every new landed home in Singapore benefits from the same six security and infrastructure layers. The difference in a new build is that all six can be fully integrated from day one — with no compromises on coverage, aesthetics, or cabling routes.

📷

Surveillance & Detection

ESSENTIAL

IP cameras at every entry point, perimeter corners, and driveway. NVR pre-wired to a dedicated cabinet. Cable routes chosen for ideal coverage — not for surface-mount convenience. AI motion detection to eliminate false alerts from foliage and pets.

Alarm System

ESSENTIAL

PIR detectors, door contacts, and glass break sensors on every ground floor opening. Panel in an accessible location with battery backup. Siren positions chosen for coverage and aesthetics. Wireless or hybrid wired/wireless depending on the construction type.

Video Intercom

ESSENTIAL

Video door station at the main gate and front door — wired back to an indoor monitor and connected to the homeowner's smartphone. Visitors can be seen, spoken to, and let in from anywhere. Back-boxes recessed into pillars and walls during construction for a flush finish.

Access Control

ESSENTIAL

Fingerprint or card readers at the main pedestrian gate and front door. Back-box recessed into the pillar or door frame during construction for a flush finish. Temporary contractor PINs during the construction period, replaced at handover.

Gate Automation

ESSENTIAL

Underground conduit for motor power and signal cables — runs that cross driveways and paths that will be paved after completion. Vehicle gates use sliding or underground swing motors depending on weight and aesthetics. Both require UPS battery backup. Conduit and motor pits must be cast during structural stage.

WiFi & Network

ESSENTIAL

Pre-Construction Checklist

Five Things to Tell Your Architect Before Walls Go Up

Most architects in Singapore are not security specialists — and they should not need to be. But there are five things your architect needs to know before structural drawings are finalised, to avoid decisions that constrain your security system later.

1

Where the NVR and Alarm Panel Will Be Located

These need a dedicated electrical circuit, network connection, and adequate ventilation. A utility room, store room, or built-in cabinet near the main DB board is ideal — decide this before wall layouts are fixed.

2

Camera Positions on Every External Elevation

Each camera position needs a back-box, a conduit run to the NVR location, and an adjacent power point. Mark these on the elevation drawings before the structural stage — repositioning after plastering means hacking.

3

Underground Conduit for Gate Motors

Gate motor cables and intercom cables should run underground in conduit from both gateposts to the main house — surface conduit on finished gateposts is visible and vulnerable. Plan the underground route before landscaping is designed.

4

Network Switch Location and Cat6 Cabling

IP cameras and IP intercoms need a network switch. Plan its location alongside the NVR and ensure Cat6 cable runs to each camera and intercom position during first fix — retrofitting structured cabling through a finished house is expensive and disruptive.

5

Gate Pillar Dimensions for Access Readers

If a fingerprint or card reader is planned at the gate, the pillar needs to be deep enough for a recessed back-box — minimum 150mm depth. Surface-mounted readers on narrow pillars are a common and easily avoided aesthetic problem.

Common Questions

Questions We Hear From Homeowners and Architects

When is the right time to contact Securevision on a new build?

The ideal time is when structural drawings are being finalised — before the main contractor starts on site. This gives us time to review the plans, advise on equipment locations, and prepare a wiring schedule for first fix. We can join later, but earlier is always better.

Do we need to involve Securevision before appointing the main contractor?

Not necessarily, but it helps. If you can share the structural drawings with us before the main contractor's preliminary meeting, we can provide equipment locations and conduit specifications that the main contractor prices into their preliminaries — avoiding variations later in the programme.

Can you work with any main contractor?

Yes. We operate as a specialist sub-contractor and coordinate directly with the main contractor's site manager and M&E sub-contractor. We provide our own materials, our own tools, and our own supervision. We do not require the main contractor to manage us.

What if I only want to pre-wire and decide on equipment later?

This is a common and sensible approach. We can provide a pre-wire-only service — installing conduit and draw-wires to all positions — without specifying final equipment. The system is then installed and configured after handover, using the pre-wired infrastructure. The pre-wire cost is recovered many times over in the final installation saving.

How much does pre-wiring cost?

Pre-wiring for a typical Singapore landed home ranges from $2,000 to $6,000 depending on the number of camera positions, gate motor runs, and the complexity of the cable routes. This is typically 30–40% of what the same cabling would cost as a retrofit after completion.
