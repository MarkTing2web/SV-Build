The Problem

Many Intercom Systems Are Still Running — But Already Outdated

Across many condominiums and residential developments, intercom systems installed years ago are still in operation. They may still function — but behind the surface, they are reaching the edge of failure.

No Spare Parts

Replacement parts are no longer readily available for legacy proprietary architectures.

No Vendor Support

Manufacturers have often discontinued support for older analogue or digital models.

Slower Repairs

Repairs are becoming significantly slower and more expensive as expertise becomes scarce.

Poor Quality

Audio and video quality no longer meet current expectations for security and clarity.

The Real Risk

When Intercom Systems Fail, It Affects More Than Communication

The intercom system is not just a convenience; it is a critical part of your property’s access control layer. When it becomes unreliable, it creates operational friction that cascades through the estate:

  • Significant delays for visitors and deliveries at the entrance
  • Mounting frustration for residents who cannot verify guests
  • Heavier operational dependence on guards for manual opening
  • Critical security gaps when verification is bypassed due to system failure

In many cases, failure does not happen all at once. It degrades over time until the system can no longer be repaired.

The Repair Trap

Repairing an Obsolete System Is Often a Short-Term Fix

For older intercom systems, maintenance becomes increasingly difficult because spare parts are discontinued and system architecture is outdated or proprietary.

Each repair extends the system slightly — but increases long-term uncertainty and cost. At some point, replacement is not a choice. It becomes necessary.

Proprietary Lock-in

Old systems often use non-standard cabling or proprietary signals that don't work with modern components.

End-of-Life Status

Many legacy vendors have completely exited the intercom market, leaving no technical support desk available.

Cumulative Costs

The total cost of multiple incremental repairs often exceeds the cost of a modern, supported IP upgrade.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions we hear from homeowners, MCSTs, and managing agents evaluating an intercom upgrade.

Can I keep the existing intercom wiring when upgrading to IP video?

In many cases yes. Two-wire intercom cable can carry IP signals with the right interface hardware, avoiding the cost and disruption of full rewiring. For estates with extensive intercom networks across multiple blocks, we assess the cable condition and run a compatibility test before recommending the approach. If rewiring is genuinely necessary, we plan it in phases to minimise disruption to residents.

Can residents receive intercom calls on their mobile phone rather than a handset?

Yes — this is a standard feature of modern IP intercom systems. Residents download the VESTA app and receive calls on their smartphone. They can answer from anywhere in Singapore or overseas, see the caller via the door camera, and release the door remotely. Physical handsets can coexist with the mobile app if some residents prefer them — the two work together on the same system.

Our analogue intercom still works. Why should we upgrade now rather than wait until it fails?

A working analogue system is not a reason to delay, but there are two practical reasons to act before failure. First, spare parts for most analogue intercom systems manufactured before 2015 are no longer available — when a component fails, the repair option disappears. Second, upgrading on your schedule allows proper planning, phased installation, and resident communication. Emergency replacement after a failure means rushed installation, higher costs, and disrupted residents.

How long does a full intercom replacement take across a large condominium?

For a typical mid-rise estate with 200–400 units and 2–3 entry points, a full intercom replacement typically takes 4–6 weeks. We phase the work block by block — each block retains working intercom throughout the installation until its new system is commissioned and tested. Residents are communicated with in advance about which days their block is being upgraded and what the temporary access arrangement is during the changeover.
