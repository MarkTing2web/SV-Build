Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions we hear regularly from dormitory operators and MOM-licensed facility managers evaluating security and compliance system upgrades.

Can your biometric system generate the attendance records MOM requires?

Yes. Our biometric turnstile systems generate timestamped entry and exit records for every resident, exportable in formats suitable for MOM submission. We configure the reporting module during installation and train your operations team on how to run and archive reports on the required schedule. The system generates records automatically — no manual compilation required.

How do you handle a dormitory with thousands of residents across multiple shift patterns?

We have deployed across large-scale dormitory sites handling thousands of daily movements across multiple shift windows. The system architecture handles concurrent access events at all entry points simultaneously, with all records consolidated in a single VESTA dashboard. We size the hardware — turnstile capacity, server load, network bandwidth — against your actual peak throughput figures during the site survey, not against a generic specification.

How quickly can worker credentials be issued and revoked as residents arrive and depart?

Biometric enrolment takes under 30 seconds per resident. Card or mobile credentials can be issued and remotely revoked in real time from the management dashboard — no need to be on-site. For large batch enrolments on new dormitory launches, we provide a streamlined enrolment workflow that allows your team to process large numbers of residents efficiently without creating queues at entry points.

What happens if the biometric system fails — can residents still enter?

All our biometric systems include a backup access method — typically card or PIN — so residents are never locked out due to a system fault. The VESTA platform also generates alerts when any access point goes offline, so your operations team can respond before it becomes a queue problem at the gate. We do not deploy single-point-of-failure configurations on critical entry points.
