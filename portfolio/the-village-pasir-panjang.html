When Legacy Systems Fail, Residents Pay the Price — Restoring Convenience at The Village

The failure of aging intercom infrastructure at Pasir Panjang forced residents into manual workarounds and disrupted daily living. Securevision redesigned the estate's access architecture — integrating intercom, lift, and vehicle systems — to restore everyday reliability and resident confidence.

What This Project Represents

A transition from reactive patchwork maintenance to a redesigned access architecture that prioritises resident experience and long-term system health.

The Challenge

A Legacy System Failing the Estate Rhythm

The existing system had become unreliable, outdated, or no longer fit for purpose. The Samsung intercom system had become partially non-functional, creating a growing gap between the development's premium architecture and its daily operational reality.

The lack of mobile access meant that residents were tethered to legacy physical handsets, while fragmented systems led to inconsistent security logs and significant audit gaps.

Critical Human Impact: Residents had to physically come down to escort visitors, especially for units with private lift access. What was meant to provide convenience had become a daily disruption.

The system was no longer aligned with how users expected to live or operate. A simple replacement of individual failing parts would not solve the broader issue.

Decision Point

A Strategic Choice for the Council

The management faced a critical decision: continue patching a failing system — or redesign how the system should function.

The council chose to move away from reactive repair and towards a redesigned, coordinated access environment that would serve the development for the decade ahead.

Our Approach

Designing a Coordinated Access Environment

Instead of treating each system as separate, Securevision approached them as a single coordinated environment.

The design goal was a cleaner, more intuitive path from the main gantry to the resident's front door. We implemented a redesign (not a simple replacement) with a phased rollout between Feb and May 2023, ensuring minimal disruption to active households.

The Solution

Three Layers of Integrated Access

The redesign focused on simplifying the journey from entry to destination across key access layers:

Layer 01

Intercom System

21 Akuvox R28A panels were deployed to replace the obsolete Samsung panels. This restored improved reliability and introduced mobile answering capabilities for all residents.

Learn more about Akuvox →

Layer 02

Lift Access Control

Using Akuvox EC32 controllers, we secured 5 blocks with private lift access. Residents can now release the lift for authorized visitors directly from their intercom or mobile app.

Layer 03

Vehicle Access (GantryGo)

A GantryGo LPR system was implemented to provide automated resident entry and improved visitor handling, eliminating the bottleneck at the main gantry.

Explore vehicle access solutions →

Ongoing Maintenance & Support

Beyond the installation, Securevision continues to maintain the intercom, CCTV, and access systems to ensure long-term reliability and system uptime for the estate.

Deployment Outcomes

Measurable Impact on Daily Living

What began as a fragmented and failing system was transformed into a reliable and coordinated environment across the estate.

  • ✓

    Escort Friction Removed: Eliminated the need for residents to escort visitors manually to the lobby or lift.

  • ✓

    System Reliability: Restored full intercom and lift access functionality across all 9 blocks.

  • ✓

    Automated Flow: Gantry operations are now automated via LPR, reducing manual logging and visitor wait times.

  • ✓

    Operational Continuity: Established an ongoing maintenance partnership to prevent future infrastructure degradation.

The Strategic Insight

When systems begin to fail across multiple areas, the issue is no longer about hardware.

It becomes a question of how the entire development is designed to function.

Real improvement comes not from replacement — but from rethinking the system as a whole.
