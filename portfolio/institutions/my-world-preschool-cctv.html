The Challenge

Standardising Safety Across a Growing Network

Early childhood centres operate under intense scrutiny from both regulators and parents. The primary challenge for MY World was the lack of standardisation across their legacy surveillance systems. Different centres had varying camera counts, recording qualities, and retention periods, making it difficult for headquarters to audit safety protocols centrally.

ECDA regulations mandate 100% visibility of all children's learning and activity zones, as well as strict data retention and privacy controls. Any system failure not only compromises child safety but also risks regulatory non-compliance for the operator.

The project required a "blueprint" design that could be adapted for different site footprints—from HDB void decks to standalone centres—while ensuring identical performance and ease of use for the centre principals and staff.

Decision Point

The "Zero-Blind-Spot" Learning Zone

During the pilot site survey, we identified that standard camera placement often missed the low-level activity zones where toddlers spend much of their time. A decision was made to depart from standard corner-mounting in favour of a more dense, low-profile deployment.

Securevision mandated a "dual-view" rule for every learning bay. This ensures that every child is visible from at least two angles, eliminating the risk of obscured activity during play or rest periods—a critical requirement for both child safety and staff accountability.

This standard has now been baked into the MY World security blueprint, providing a level of visibility that far exceeds the minimum regulatory requirements.
