What This Project Represents

This project represents the kind of condominium upgrade many older estates in Singapore now face: legacy infrastructure still works, but resident expectations have moved on. The challenge is not simply replacing hardware. It is modernising access, convenience, and management workflow without forcing a full-strip renovation across occupied units.

The Challenge

Modernising Two Separate Access Layers Without Major Estate Disruption

Legacy visitor communication no longer matched resident expectations

The original intercom arrangement was functional but limited. Residents could not visually verify visitors before granting access, and remote answering was not part of the workflow. For a luxury condominium, that gap matters. Convenience is not a superficial feature in this type of development. It affects daily deliveries, contractor visits, family access, and overall resident confidence in the estate.

The MCST needed a system that felt contemporary, improved verification at the point of entry, and reduced reliance on residents being physically beside an indoor station at the moment a visitor arrived.

L'viv Residences required a building-wide modernisation strategy that improved resident experience while respecting the realities of an occupied luxury development.

Unit door access had become restrictive instead of reliable

At the unit level, ageing fingerprint readers were creating friction. Single-mode authentication is vulnerable to wear, user inconsistency, and everyday real-world conditions. Wet fingers, skin changes, poor placement, or minor hardware deterioration all make the system feel less dependable than it should. That is especially problematic in a residential setting where access has to be intuitive, not technical.

Families also needed more flexibility for different user groups, including owners, children, helpers, and authorised guests. The legacy setup was no longer giving the estate enough range in how access could be issued and managed.

Full rewiring would have solved the problem technically, but not commercially

The biggest technical constraint was infrastructure. The estate’s original intercom cabling was based on a 2-wire arrangement. A full IP migration using fresh network cabling across 147 units would have meant intrusive works, higher labour cost, and a more difficult resident management process. In occupied condominiums, that kind of disruption is often what kills an otherwise sensible upgrade plan.

The real challenge, therefore, was not just selecting better technology. It was designing a route to modern functionality while using as much of the existing backbone as possible.

Our Approach

A Dual-System Upgrade Built Around Retrofit Practicality

Securevision approached L'viv as two connected but distinct access problems. The estate needed a modern visitor communication layer across common entrances, and it also needed a more resilient, flexible unit-level access layer for residents. Treating both needs as the same problem would have created unnecessary complexity. Treating them separately—but designing them to complement each other—produced a cleaner result.

Use the existing cabling to unlock a modern intercom layer

For the estate-wide intercom, we selected an IP-capable ecosystem but avoided the cost and disruption of a full cable replacement. By using 2-wire network conversion hardware, the project could preserve the existing wiring path while still introducing video calling, app-based answering, and a stronger front-end visitor experience.

This was the critical architectural move. It allowed the estate to adopt newer functionality without turning the project into a civil works exercise.

Design Decision

The most important decision was to treat the existing 2-wire backbone as an asset, not an obstacle. That shifted the project from “major renovation” to “intelligent retrofit”, which is often the difference between a proposal that gets approved and one that stalls.

Keep private unit access locally dependable

At the individual unit doors, Securevision specified a standalone biometric and credential approach rather than forcing everything into a single network-dependent architecture. That gave residents more authentication choices while protecting the essential requirement that front-door access must remain dependable even if other building systems are offline.

Face, palm, card, PIN, and app-based pathways created far more flexibility than the previous fingerprint-only approach, and the standalone design respected both privacy and operational resilience.

Systems Used

Systems & Equipment Deployed

The project combined a retrofit-ready video intercom architecture for common entrances with standalone multi-modal access devices at each residential unit.

System 01

Estate Video Intercom & Visitor Entry

Securevision deployed Akuvox X915 visitor panels at the estate’s key entry points together with indoor monitors and 2-wire network switches. This delivered video verification, app connectivity, and stronger visitor workflow without requiring new full-building cabling to every unit.

System 02

Standalone Unit Door Access

Each residential unit received a ZKTeco MiniAC Plus reader with multiple credential options including face, palm, card, PIN, and mobile app support. The aim was to improve daily usability while keeping front-door access dependable at the unit level.

Measured Outcomes

What Changed After Deployment

The measurable outcomes below reflect the clear structural improvements achieved through the upgrade. Where direct operational metrics were not formally tracked, the project itself still demonstrates meaningful engineering and implementation gains.

147

All residential units were brought into the upgrade scope, giving the estate a consistent modernised access experience rather than a partial rollout.

5

Residents gained five authentication paths at their unit doors, reducing dependence on a single biometric mode and improving everyday usability.

26

The use of 26 network conversion units made it possible to bridge legacy 2-wire infrastructure into a more modern intercom architecture.

Why This Matters to Similar Sites

For many Singapore condominiums, the real blocker is not recognising the need to upgrade. It is fear of cost, disruption, and infrastructure limitations. L'viv shows that an estate can move forward decisively when the upgrade path is engineered around existing realities instead of ignoring them.
