After Installation — Who Do You Call and What to Expect?

The installation is done. The technician has left. You have a system you are not entirely sure how to use, and a vague sense that something eventually needs attention. Here is what the post-installation relationship with your security company should look like.

1. What should happen on the day of installation

Before the technician leaves, three things should have happened. If any was missed, call for a follow-up visit:

  • Full Walk-through: Every user in the household must be shown how to arm, disarm, and check zone status. App-connected users should have their access tested.
  • Walk-test: The technician must deliberately trigger each zone — opening doors, windows, and walking through motion sensors — to confirm that both the panel and app register signals correctly.
  • Documentation: You should receive a zone list (Zone 1 = front door, etc.), all user and master PINs, and clear warranty terms for parts and labour.

A proper handover means every user in the household understands how to operate the system.

2. The warranty — what it covers

Professional installations carry a one-year warranty on parts and labour for installation defects. This does NOT cover physical damage after installation, consumable batteries, power surges/lightning, or third-party modifications.

Firmware updates: Ask your installer if your system receives regular updates and if they should be handled by an integrator or automatically over the network.

3. Who to call and when

  • Routine usage questions: Your installer should be reachable during business hours. This shouldn't be a chargeable service call.
  • Fault alerts: Check the keypad or app (battery, communication, or zone tamper are common). If it doesn't clear after a reset, call for support.
  • Alarm activation: Review logs first for the trigger zone and cross-reference with CCTV. If the cause is unclear, investigate with your installer rather than just resetting.
  • Component failure: A camera going offline or a keypad becoming unresponsive is a standard service call.

KEY POINT

Save your installer's contact details in your phone on the day of installation. The moment you need support is the most inconvenient time to search for a business card.

4. Service plans — do you need one?

Scheduled maintenance visits involve walk-tests, battery replacements, and hardware health checks. For complex systems, a service plan — such as Securevision's from $400 per year — ensures professional reviews annually. Simple systems can often be checked by homeowners using basic diagnostic tools.

Annual maintenance visits catch battery degradation and component wear before they cause problems.

5. When your installer is no longer available

If your original provider is gone: first, document every model and NVR/panel detail. Second, contact the brand's local distributor for an authorised referral. Third, contact a licensed security integrator. Police Licence holders listed with the SPF can take over support for most standard brands.
