Upgrade Your Security System vs Starting Fresh

The honest answer is: sometimes yes, sometimes no, and sometimes it depends on a question most people have not thought to ask. Here is how to work out which situation you are in.

1. The question behind the question

When a homeowner asks whether they can upgrade parts of an existing system rather than replace everything, they are usually asking one of three things:

  • Can I add more cameras to my existing CCTV system without replacing the NVR?
  • Can I add new detectors to my existing burglar alarm without replacing the panel?
  • Can I keep my existing cabling and just swap out the old equipment?

Each of these has a different answer. Compatibility is the determining factor, and it requires a professional assessment of what is currently installed.

2. CCTV systems — what can and cannot be reused

IP Compatibility (ONVIF):

Modern IP cameras and NVRs use standard protocols (primarily ONVIF). In principle, brands can be mixed, but full feature compatibility — like AI human detection — typically requires staying within the same manufacturer ecosystem.

NVR Channel Limit:

Every NVR has a fixed channel limit (e.g., 4, 8, 16). A 4-channel NVR cannot support a 5th camera without an upgrade. Expanding beyond your current limit usually requires a new NVR.

Hard Disk & Cabling:

Hard disks should be replaced every three years. Existing Cat5e/Cat6 cabling is usually reusable for new IP cameras. However, old analogue coaxial cabling is generally not suitable for modern IP systems without significant compromises.

3. Burglar alarm systems — what can and cannot be reused

Wired detectors (PIR, door contacts) are broadly compatible across brands. Wireless detectors, however, are strictly brand-specific — a RISCO sensor will not work on a Paradox panel.

Individual faulty sensors can often be replaced without changing the core panel. However, if your panel is at its zone capacity or the manufacturer no longer supports it, starting fresh is usually the more reliable path.

Wired detectors are broadly compatible across alarm brands. If your existing cable runs are intact and the hardware is in good condition, they can typically be reused.

4. Intercom systems — the compatibility challenge

Intercoms are the most difficult to partially upgrade. Traditional wired systems use proprietary protocols. Swapping just the door station rarely works with a different brand's indoor monitor. IP intercoms (like Akuvox) use open SIP standards, which allow for better modularity, but integrated proprietary systems usually require a full swap for a meaningful upgrade.

5. The assessment — what a good installer will check

  • Current panel/NVR firmware and remaining zone/channel capacity.
  • Existing cabling condition and compatibility.
  • Backup battery health and NVR hard disk status.

KEY POINT

Adding new cameras to an NVR with a failing hard disk is a wasted investment. Always ensure the foundation is healthy before expanding.

6. When to upgrade parts and when to start fresh

Upgrade parts when: The system is from a reputable brand, under 7 years old, and has spare capacity.

Start fresh when: The system is obsolete (over 10 years), the brand is unsupported, or you need to switch from analogue to IP infrastructure.

We prefer to extend existing systems where it makes sense to save costs. But we won't build on a weak foundation. A brief assessment visit—offered without charge—is enough to give you a clear answer.
