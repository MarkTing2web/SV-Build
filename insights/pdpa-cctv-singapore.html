PDPA and CCTV in Singapore: What Property Owners and Businesses Need to Know

Installing CCTV is straightforward. Managing it legally is where most property owners fall short. Here is what the Personal Data Protection Act actually requires — and what it does not.

Does PDPA Apply to Your CCTV System?

The short answer is yes — if your cameras capture footage of identifiable individuals, the Personal Data Protection Act 2012 (PDPA) applies. Video recordings that show a person's face, gait, or other identifying features constitute personal data under Singapore law.

The Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC), which administers and enforces the PDPA, has addressed CCTV use specifically in its Advisory Guidelines on Selected Topics (revised May 2024). These guidelines make clear that organisations operating CCTV systems have data protection obligations — even if the cameras are installed purely for security purposes.

There is one important exception: CCTV used solely for personal or domestic purposes is not subject to the PDPA. A homeowner monitoring their own gate is not covered. A condominium management committee monitoring common areas is.

There is no requirement to register your CCTV system with the PDPC before installation.

The Notification Obligation: You Need Signage

One of the most frequently overlooked requirements is notification. The PDPC's guidelines state that notices should be placed at locations where individuals would have sufficient awareness that CCTV is in operation and for what purpose.

In practice this means:

  • Signage at entry points to areas under surveillance, indicating that CCTV is in use
  • A stated purpose on the signage — for example, "CCTV is operated for security and safety purposes"
  • Placement that is visible before an individual enters the surveilled area — not once they are already inside

The guidelines also address covert surveillance: if notifying people of CCTV coverage would defeat its purpose — for example, monitoring a cash handling area for internal theft — the PDPC allows for a different approach, but the organisation must still document its purpose and apply appropriate data protection measures to the footage.

How Long Can You Keep the Footage?

The PDPA does not prescribe a specific retention period for CCTV footage. What it does require is that personal data — including video recordings — should not be retained longer than is necessary for the purpose for which it was collected.

In practice, the widely followed standard in Singapore is 30 days for most commercial and residential properties, consistent with police recommendations. Beyond 30 days, you need a documented reason. Sector-specific rules layer on top of this baseline:

  • Workplace safety incidents (WSH): footage relevant to an incident must be retained for 180 days
  • Active police investigations: preserve footage until the investigation concludes
  • Construction sites (contract value above S$5 million): 30 days standard, 180 days for safety incidents — mandatory from June 2024
Key Point

The 30-day figure comes from police recommendation, not a hard legislative mandate. However, retaining footage beyond 30 days without a documented operational reason creates PDPA exposure — you are holding personal data longer than is necessary for the stated purpose. A written internal policy that specifies your retention period and the reason behind it demonstrates accountability under the PDPA.

If you extend retention beyond the default period, document the reason. Configuring your NVR to overwrite after 30 days and recording this policy in writing satisfies both the PDPA's data minimisation principle and practical operational needs.

Who Can Access the Footage?

CCTV footage should only be accessible to authorised personnel. The PDPA's Protection Obligation requires organisations to implement reasonable security measures to protect personal data against unauthorised access, disclosure, copying, use, or modification.

For CCTV systems this means:

  • Restricting NVR and VMS access to named personnel on a need-to-know basis
  • Securing login credentials — change default factory passwords at commissioning and update them when staff leave
  • Logging access where the system supports it
  • Securing physical access to the NVR or server room

Beyond internal access, individuals have the right under the PDPA to request access to footage in which they appear. Organisations must respond to such requests, though they are permitted to mask or obscure other individuals captured in the same footage to protect their privacy.

Police may also request footage in the course of an investigation. Complying with a lawful police request does not constitute a PDPA violation. Sharing footage with third parties for purposes unrelated to the original security purpose — for example, posting it publicly or sharing with a neighbouring building — requires consent or a clear legal basis.

Camera Placement: What Falls Outside Your Boundaries?

The PDPC guidelines address an issue that arises frequently in Singapore's dense built environment: cameras that inadvertently or deliberately capture footage beyond the operator's own premises.

If your cameras capture footage of public areas, neighbouring properties, or common areas not under your management, you are collecting personal data beyond the scope of your stated purpose. The PDPC requires organisations in this situation to take reasonable steps — such as adjusting camera angles, using privacy masking features, or limiting the field of view — to minimise capture of areas outside their boundary.

This applies to:

  • Landed residential properties whose cameras capture the public road or neighbouring land
  • Commercial premises with cameras pointing towards entrances shared with adjacent businesses
  • Industrial facilities whose perimeter cameras capture public walkways

A camera positioned to cover a carpark entrance will inevitably capture some footpath. This is generally accepted as incidental. Deliberately targeting areas outside your premises is a different matter.

Common Compliance Gaps

These are the areas most frequently not addressed in Singapore commercial and residential CCTV installations:

  • No signage, or signage placed after entry — the obligation requires notice before individuals enter the surveilled area
  • Default NVR credentials never changed — many systems are deployed with factory-set passwords that are never updated, a straightforward data security failure
  • No written retention policy — footage is kept until the drive is full and overwrites, with no documented basis for the period used
  • Footage shared informally — screenshots or video clips shared via WhatsApp among committee members or management staff without a clear need-to-know basis
  • Cameras covering sensitive areas — toilets, changing rooms, and areas where individuals have a reasonable expectation of privacy cannot be covered under any circumstances

What a Compliant Installation Looks Like

A PDPA-compliant CCTV installation is not significantly more complex than a standard one. The technical elements are identical — the difference is in the documentation and operational procedures around it. The key elements are:

  1. Purpose documented — a written statement of why CCTV is installed and what it is used for
  2. Signage in place — visible at entry points to surveilled areas, with purpose stated
  3. Retention policy set and configured — NVR set to overwrite at the agreed interval, documented in writing
  4. Access restricted — login credentials secured and limited to authorised personnel
  5. Camera angles reviewed — confirmed to cover only the intended areas, with incidental external capture minimised
  6. Incident procedure in place — a clear internal process for footage retrieval, police requests, and individual access requests

None of these require legal expertise to implement. They require deliberate setup at the point of installation — which is the right time to do it.

Securevision's View

PDPA compliance for CCTV is largely an installation and commissioning discipline, not an ongoing legal burden. The decisions that matter — camera placement, retention configuration, access credentials, signage — are made when the system goes in. Getting them right at that point costs no more than getting them wrong. Getting them wrong surfaces as a liability later.

What to Discuss With Your Installer

PDPA compliance should be part of the commissioning conversation, not something you work out afterwards. When engaging any security contractor for a CCTV installation, these are worth raising:

  • Retention configuration — confirm the NVR will be set to overwrite at your agreed retention period, and ask for this to be noted in the handover documentation
  • Camera angle review — a professional installer should walk through final camera positions with you and flag any angles that capture beyond your boundary
  • Signage placement — signage is often left to the client, but your installer should advise on placement relative to the surveilled area
  • Access credentials — confirm default NVR credentials will be changed at commissioning and that you receive the updated login details in writing
  • Handover documentation — a proper handover should include system documentation sufficient to support your own internal compliance records

These are not complex requirements. An experienced integrator will have a process for each of them. If they do not, that is worth knowing before the cameras go up.
