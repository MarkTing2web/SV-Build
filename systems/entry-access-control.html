Two Systems. One Purpose.

Entry & Access Is Not One Thing — It Is Two

Most properties need two distinct systems that work together — an access control system that verifies identity and controls door hardware, and an intercom system that manages visitor communication and remote entry. Each is effective on its own. Together, they give you complete control over who enters, when, and from where.

Access Control — Identity & Door Management

Access control verifies identity at every entry point using face recognition, palm vein, fingerprint, card, or PIN — and controls the door hardware based on the result. Every entry and exit is logged with a timestamp and identity record, giving you a complete audit trail across your property.

IP Intercom — Visitor Communication & Remote Entry

An IP intercom handles the visitor experience — two-way video and audio between the visitor at the door and the resident, guard, or receptionist. The person answering can see who is there, speak to them, and release the door remotely from a phone, tablet, or guard workstation without being physically at the entrance.

Managed Together on One Platform

When both systems are IP-connected and integrated on a platform like VESTA or the access controller's own software, credentials, audit trails, and door status are managed from a single dashboard. A visitor call on the intercom triggers the access controller to log the entry — one event, one record, one system.

System 1 of 2 — Access Control

From Keys and Cards to Multi-Modal Identity

The shift in access control is not just from mechanical to digital — it is from single-credential to multi-modal identity. The right credential type depends on the property, the risk level, and the user population.

Traditional Access — Keys & Basic Cards

  • Physical keys can be lost, duplicated, or passed on without accountability
  • Basic proximity cards offer no identity verification — anyone with the card enters
  • Zero audit trail — no record of who entered, when, or how many times
  • Revoking access requires collecting the key or card physically
  • No scheduling — access is all-or-nothing, 24 hours a day
  • Cannot integrate with CCTV or intercom systems

Modern Access — Multi-Modal Identity

  • Face recognition — touchless, fast, no credential to carry or lose
  • Palm vein — contactless biometric with very low false acceptance rate
  • Fingerprint — established biometric for high-throughput access points
  • Smart card — encrypted credential with identity binding, not just a token
  • Mobile credential — phone-based access via Bluetooth or NFC
  • Full audit trail — every event logged with identity, timestamp, and door

Choosing the Right Credential

No single credential type is right for every property. Face recognition is ideal for high-traffic lobbies where touchless speed matters. Palm vein suits hygiene-sensitive environments like healthcare. Fingerprint works well for staff access in commercial and industrial sites. Smart cards remain the standard for large multi-tenant buildings. We specify the credential type based on your user population, throughput requirements, and integration needs — not on what is easiest to install.

System 2 of 2 — IP Intercom

From Buzzers to Video-Verified Remote Entry

The intercom system is often the first point of contact between a visitor and your property. Modern IP intercoms have transformed this from a simple audio buzz to a full video-verified, remotely managed entry experience — accessible from anywhere on any device.

Old Model — Analogue Intercom

  • Audio only — no way to see who is at the door
  • Fixed indoor station — must be at the panel to answer
  • One-to-one call only — cannot forward to multiple people
  • No integration with access control or CCTV systems
  • No record of calls, failed entries, or visitor activity
  • Wired infrastructure — difficult to expand or relocate

New Model — IP Video Intercom

  • HD video and two-way audio — see and speak to the visitor clearly
  • Answer from any device — phone, tablet, PC, or guard workstation
  • Remote door release — unlock from anywhere, no need to be on-site
  • Integrated with access control — visitor entry logged as an access event
  • Call forwarding and simultaneous ring to multiple devices or users
  • Full call log — every visitor interaction recorded with timestamp

What the Intercom Does That Access Control Cannot

An access control system manages credentialled users — people already registered in the system. It cannot handle an unregistered visitor, a delivery driver, or a contractor arriving for the first time. That is the intercom's role — to create a managed communication point between an unknown visitor and a responsible person who can verify identity visually and decide whether to grant entry. Together, the two systems handle every type of entry: credentialled and uncredentialled.

Operational Reality

How Both Systems Work in Real Life

These scenarios show each system doing what it does best — and what happens when both work together.

Staff Entry at Office — Face Recognition

An employee walks up to the lobby turnstile. The face recognition reader identifies them in under a second, releases the gate, and logs the entry. No card to tap, no PIN to remember. The system records who entered, at what time, and at which door — automatically.

Visitor at Condominium — IP Intercom

A visitor presses the intercom at the guardhouse. The resident receives a video call on their phone, sees the visitor clearly, speaks to them, and taps to release the lobby door — from their apartment two floors up, or from their office across town.

Revoked Access — Instant Lockout

A contractor's engagement ends. Their credential is revoked in the access control dashboard immediately — from any device, in seconds. The next time they present their card or face at any door, access is denied. No key to collect, no lock to change, no site visit required.

Healthcare Ward — Zone Restriction

A healthcare facility restricts ward access to registered nursing staff only. The access control system enforces zone permissions by role — admin staff can enter the lobby and offices but not the wards. Any attempt to access a restricted zone is logged and flagged for review.

How Access and Intercom Work With Other Systems

  • Access event triggers camera pop-up: When a door is opened or an access attempt is denied, the guard's monitor automatically displays the camera covering that entry point — no hunting for the right feed.
  • Intercom call linked to CCTV: When a visitor calls the intercom, the relevant camera feed appears alongside the call — the guard sees both the intercom panel view and the wider camera coverage simultaneously.
  • Credentials sync across systems: Resident or staff credentials registered in the access control system can be synchronised with vehicle LPR and management platforms — one identity across all entry layers.
  • Single dashboard via platform: VESTA or the access controller's own software displays door status, live intercom calls, and access events in one operational view — guard sees the full picture without switching between systems.

Deployment

Where These Systems Are Used

Access control and intercom are deployed across every property type — the configuration and credential mix varies by the operational model and user population.

Condominiums & Managed Living

IP intercom at the main lobby and guardhouse with resident app release. Face or card access at lift lobbies and facilities. Private lift control restricts floor access to unit residents only.

Offices & Commercial

Face recognition at turnstiles and main entry. Visitor management intercom at the reception. Zone-based access by department or clearance level. Lift control restricts floor access by staff role.

Schools & Institutions

Intercom at the main gate with visitor logging at the reception. Staff biometric access at entrances. Student movement restricted to designated zones during school hours.

Healthcare Facilities

Strict zone control by staff role — wards, medication rooms, and restricted areas access-controlled by biometric credential. Intercom at all visitor entry points with call recording.
