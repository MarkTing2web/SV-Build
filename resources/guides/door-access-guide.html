Access Control Fundamentals

1. What Is a Door Access Control System?

Door access control systems are electronic security solutions that replace traditional mechanical keys with credentials like cards, fingerprints, or mobile phones. They give you complete control over who can enter your premises and when.

Why Keys Are Insecure

Traditional mechanical keys have fundamental security weaknesses that make them unsuitable for modern security needs:

  • Easy duplication: Any key can be copied at a locksmith in minutes. There is no way to know if your key has been duplicated by a contractor or departing employee.
  • Irrevocable access: When an employee leaves or a key is lost, the only solution is to change the entire lock and reissue keys to everyone — expensive and time-consuming.
  • No audit trail: You have no way to know who entered, when they entered, or which door they used. If something goes missing, you cannot investigate who had access.
  • No time restrictions: A key works 24/7. You cannot restrict access to office hours only or prevent weekend entry.
  • Key management complexity: As your organisation grows, key distribution becomes a logistical challenge. Who has which keys? How many copies exist?

How Door Access Control Solves These Problems

  • Complete audit trail: Every door access is logged with timestamp, user name, and door location.
  • Instant credential revocation: When an employee leaves, disable their credential in seconds — no lock changes required.
  • Time-based access restrictions: Grant access only during specific hours. Cleaning staff can access all areas 6 AM to 8 AM only.
  • No authorised duplication: Access cards cannot be duplicated without proper encoding equipment. Biometric credentials cannot be copied at all.
  • Centralised management: Add or remove users from a single interface. Generate reports showing who has access to what.

The Four Core Components

An access control system consists of four elements working in sequence:

  • Credentials: What identifies the person — access card, fingerprint, facial scan, PIN, or mobile phone.
  • Readers: Scan the credential and send the data to the controller. Mounted outside the door.
  • Controllers: Validate the credential against the database, check access level and time, and send the unlock signal.
  • Locks: Electrified locks that release when the controller sends the unlock signal.

The complete sequence — present credential, reader scans, controller validates, lock releases — happens in 200–500 milliseconds.

System Architecture

2. How Door Access Systems Work

Understanding how access control components work together helps you make informed decisions about system design, capabilities, and limitations.

The Five Core Components

1

Credentials — Your Identity

Credentials identify who is requesting access. Modern systems support multiple types simultaneously: access cards (EM 125kHz, MIFARE 13.56MHz, or high-security DESFire AES-128), biometrics (fingerprint, facial, palm vein), mobile access (BLE, NFC, QR), and PIN codes. A single system can support cards, biometrics, and mobile access at the same time.

2

Readers — The Scanner

Readers scan credentials and send data to the controller. Card readers use radio frequency (RF) to energise the card and read its ID. Reading distances range from 3–10cm for standard proximity to up to 2 metres for long-range vehicle access. Readers connect to controllers via RS-485 or Wiegand wired connections for maximum reliability.

3

Controllers — The Brain

Controllers are the intelligence of the system. They check: Is the credential valid? Does this user have access to this door? Is the current time within permitted hours? If all pass, they send an unlock signal and log the event. Network controllers connect to a central server or cloud platform — standalone units act independently for single-door setups.

4

Locks — The Physical Barrier

Electrified locks secure the door and release on signal. EM locks use powerful magnets (300kg–1,200kg) and are fail-safe (unlock without power — essential for fire safety). Electric strikes replace the strike plate and are available in fail-safe and fail-secure versions. Drop bolts suit high-security glass or double doors. Lock selection depends on door type, swing direction, and SCDF fire safety requirements.

5

Management Software

The interface for administrators to configure users, assign access levels, and generate audit reports. On-premise software keeps data locally with no subscription fees. Cloud-based software enables management from any web browser or mobile app — popular for ease of use and remote accessibility. Both provide comprehensive audit trails, real-time monitoring, and automated reporting.

Standalone Systems

4. Single Door Access Control

Single door access control systems are self-contained reader-controller units ideal for controlling one to three doors without centralised management requirements.

Unlike multi-door systems that separate readers from central controllers, single-door systems integrate everything in one device. The unit contains the scanner, logic, user database, and lock relay. It mounts directly beside the door, eliminating extensive cabling back to a server room.

Scalable Systems

5. Multi-Door Access Control

Multi-door systems use separate controllers and readers to manage 4–250+ doors with centralised management, comprehensive audit trails, and deep integration capabilities.

Multi-door systems separate the intelligence (controllers) from the interface (readers). Controllers are installed in secure internal locations like IT rooms. Readers are simple scanners mounted outside doors. This architecture means even if a reader is physically attacked or removed, the controller — and access decision logic — remains safe inside the protected area.

Advanced Features Available Only on Multi-Door Systems

  • Anti-Passback: Prevents credential sharing — a user must log an exit before the same card can be used to enter again.
  • Door Interlock (Mantrap): Only one door in a pair can be open at a time — prevents piggybacking into high-security areas.
  • Access Levels: Assign permissions by role — Staff access general offices, Executive level accesses server rooms and finance suites.
  • Event-Triggered Actions: Forced door entry triggers CCTV recording. Fire alarm activation unlocks all exit doors automatically.

6. Upgrading from Mechanical Locks to Access Control

Most businesses already have doors secured with traditional mechanical locks. Upgrading to electronic access control does not always require replacing doors or frames — in many cases you can retrofit access control onto existing infrastructure.

Assessing Your Current Setup

Door Material Retrofit Suitability Notes
TimberEasyAccepts most lock types — electric strike is the standard choice
MetalEasyEM lock on frame or electric strike both work well
AluminiumGoodMay require reinforcement for heavy 600kg+ EM locks
Frameless GlassSpecialistRequires U-bracket EM lock — no drilling in the glass
UPVC / CompositeCare neededInternal core must be assessed before drilling

Phased Implementation Approach

Phased upgrades spread costs while providing immediate security benefits at your most vulnerable points:

  • Phase 1 — Main Entrances: Immediately control who enters the building. Revoke ex-staff access instantly and track all visitors.
  • Phase 2 — Secondary Access: Secure side doors, loading bays, and parking gates to prevent tailgating or unauthorised entry.
  • Phase 3 — Restricted Internal Zones: Control internal movement for server rooms, finance departments, and executive suites.

Common Retrofit Challenges

Challenge Solution
No power near the doorUse Power over Ethernet (PoE) — data and power over a single Cat 6 cable. Or wireless battery-powered locks for low-traffic internal doors.

Outswing doorElectric strike or shear lock — do not use standard EM lock on an outswing door without verifying the mounting geometry first.

Instant Revocation — The Absolute Benefit

The primary advantage over mechanical keys is the ability to instantly revoke access. When an employee leaves, their credential is deactivated in the software in seconds, reflecting across all networked doors immediately.

Best Practice

Deactivate credentials during the exit interview process. Never wait until end of day or the following morning to secure your premises. Also conduct a full user database audit quarterly — deactivate any temporary contractors, ex-employees, and expired visitor cards.

Hardware Selection

9. Reader Types Explained

Readers are the physical interface between users and the access control system. Choosing the right reader type depends on your credential technology, security requirements, and environmental conditions.

RFID Card Readers

  • EM Readers (125kHz): Basic, reliable, low cost. Suitable for residential gates and internal office doors where basic proximity sensing is sufficient. Vulnerable to card cloning — not recommended for commercial security.
  • MIFARE / Smart Readers (13.56MHz): Support encrypted communication. Highly recommended for commercial suites to prevent card cloning.
  • Multi-Technology Readers: Support both legacy EM and modern MIFARE simultaneously — ideal for phased technology migrations.

Electrified Locks

10. Lock Types & Selection

The electric lock is the physical strength of your system. Choosing the right lock depends on the door material, swing direction, and fire safety requirements of your specific location.

Electromagnetic (EM) Locks

EM locks use powerful magnetic force to hold a door closed. They are inherently fail-safe — unlock when power is cut — making them the gold standard for fire exit routes. Three key specifications: 300kg (internal office doors), 600kg (commercial entrances, industry standard), and 1,200kg (high-security perimeter gates).

For frameless glass doors, U-bracket EM locks clamp onto the top header without any drilling in the tempered glass — the only compliant solution for glass doors in Singapore commercial properties.

Modern Deployment

12. Cloud-Based Access Control

Cloud-based access control eliminates the need for on-site servers, offering remote management, automatic updates, and multi-site capabilities through internet-connected readers and secure cloud platforms.

Traditional systems rely on local servers. Cloud systems connect readers directly to platforms like AWS or Azure — intelligence moves from your server room to the cloud, enabling true anywhere management. Add or revoke users via mobile app with credentials syncing across all sites in under 30 seconds. Grant access to delivery drivers from your smartphone even when out of the office.

Offline Intelligence — What If Internet Fails?

Modern cloud readers are edge-intelligent. Readers store up to 10,000 recently used credentials in local memory — if the internet goes down, existing employees can still enter normally. Access decisions are made at the door. Once connection is restored, the reader uploads all offline logs and downloads any new user changes made during the outage. For high-uptime requirements, we deploy readers with 4G/LTE failover SIM slots.

14. Access Levels, Time Groups & Permissions Architecture

Permissions are built on a layered architecture of Access Levels and Time Groups. This structure enables sophisticated security policies that scale effortlessly without manual per-user configuration.

Hierarchy of Permission

Layer Defines Example
Access LevelsWHERE a user can go"Staff" = Main entrance + pantry + meeting rooms
Time GroupsWHEN access is allowed"Office Hours" = Monday–Friday, 08:00–18:00
Door GroupsLogical zones combining multiple doors"Public Areas" = 5 separate lobby doors assigned as one group
Best Practice — Principle of Least Privilege

Always start by granting the minimum access required for an employee's job function. It is much easier to add permissions later than to identify and revoke excessive access after a security breach. Conduct a full access level audit at least annually.

Dual-Purpose System

15. Time & Attendance Integration

Access control systems can double as high-precision time and attendance platforms, automatically tracking work hours as employees enter and exit — eliminating manual timesheets and ensuring MOM-compliant payroll records.

Every door tap becomes a time punch. The first entry of the day is logged as the clock-in; the last exit is the clock-out. The system calculates total hours automatically. Under the Singapore Employment Act, employers must maintain accurate records of work hours for at least two years — our systems automate this record-keeping for audit readiness.

Solving Buddy Punching

Time theft through buddy punching — clocking in for a friend — can cost a 100-staff factory upwards of S$18,000 per year. Biometric verification (fingerprint or facial recognition) ensures that every time punch corresponds to the actual physical presence of the employee. This is the primary reason manufacturing and logistics clients in Singapore specify biometric terminals at shift gates rather than card readers.

17. Advanced Integrations

Modern access control systems act as the bridge between security, facility management, and life safety systems, creating a unified, responsive building environment.

CCTV and Video Verification

Link every door scan to a camera view. In high-security environments, a video popup on the security desk allows for instant visual verification of the entrant, preventing card sharing and tailgating. Forced door entry can automatically trigger recording on the associated camera.

Visitor Management

Hosts email a time-limited QR code to guests. Visitors scan the code at the entrance terminal for seamless self-entry. Automated sign-in kiosks capture visitor photos and notify the host via SMS upon arrival. Guest credentials automatically expire at a set time, ensuring no legacy access lingers after a meeting ends.

Lift and Elevator Control

Restricts floor buttons within the elevator cabin based on the user's credential. Essential for high-rise residential privacy and multi-tenant commercial buildings — staff can only reach their authorised floors, and visitors cannot wander beyond the lobby level.

Car Park Barriers and ANPR

Automatic Number Plate Recognition (ANPR) cameras read vehicle licence plates in milliseconds, lifting boom barriers for season parkers without requiring a card tap. A completely hands-free entry experience for residents and staff — the system recognises the vehicle and the barrier raises automatically.

Fire Alarm and Emergency Override

Integration with the building's Fire Alarm Panel is mandatory. Upon alarm activation, the access control system instantly releases all magnetic locks in the means of escape, allowing unimpeded evacuation in compliance with SCDF fire safety codes. Our controllers are wired directly to the Fire Alarm Panel via dry-contact relay — when the alarm triggers, power to all EM locks is cut at the source.

Emergency Procedures

22. Fire Safety & Compliance

Access control systems must never impede emergency evacuation. In Singapore, SCDF fire codes mandate strict integration between security hardware and life safety systems.

SCDF Mandatory Compliance — Fail-Safe Requirement

All doors located in a Means of Escape must use fail-safe locks. This ensures that upon fire alarm activation or power failure, all magnetic locks release immediately to allow unimpeded evacuation. Fail-secure locks are strictly prohibited on fire exit doors unless a manual mechanical override is provided. Non-compliance can result in SCDF enforcement action and invalidation of your building's fire certificate.

Fire Alarm Integration

Our controllers are wired directly to the building's Fire Alarm Panel (FAP) via a dry-contact relay. When the alarm triggers, the 12V or 24V power to all exit door locks is cut at the source — guaranteeing that occupants can exit without fumbling for cards or fingerprints during an emergency. This is a hardwired, not software-based, connection — it operates even if the access control server is offline.

Lessons Learned

24. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake What Goes Wrong The Correct Approach
Under-specifying system capacity A 2-door controller with no spare capacity requires expensive hardware replacement when the company adds one more door. Always plan for 20% future expansion when selecting controller models.
Wrong lock for outswing doors Installing a standard EM lock on an outswing door is a common site survey error — the magnet and armature plate cannot meet properly. Always confirm door swing direction before ordering hardware. Outswing doors need shear locks or surface-mount EM locks with extended brackets.
No drip loop on outdoor readers Rain travels along the cable directly into the reader electronics. Very common in Singapore's weather. Always ensure a downward drip loop is made in the cable run before it enters the reader body during installation.
Default admin passwords left unchanged Using default "admin/1234" credentials is an open invitation for network intrusion. Change all default passwords during commissioning. Document credentials securely.

Common Questions

26. Frequently Asked Questions

Question Answer
What happens to my doors during a power failure? Depends on lock type. Fail-safe doors (EM locks) unlock immediately for safety. Fail-secure doors stay locked. We recommend installing a UPS to keep systems powered for 2–4 hours during an outage.
Can I use my existing door or must I replace the frame? Most timber and metal doors can be retrofitted. We install electric strikes into existing frames or mount EM locks to the top header without replacing the door structure.
Is cloud access control more expensive than on-premise? Cloud has lower upfront cost (no server) but requires a monthly subscription. On-premise has higher initial CapEx but minimal ongoing software costs. Cloud is typically more cost-effective for 1–5 door setups and multi-branch deployments.
How often should I audit my user database? We recommend a full audit every quarter. Ensure ex-employees, expired contractors, and temporary visitor cards are permanently deactivated.
Can I integrate with my existing CCTV system? In most cases yes — major access control platforms support ONVIF and direct integration with Hikvision, Dahua, Axis, and other leading CCTV brands. We assess compatibility during site survey.
