An auto gate is one of those things that is easy to take for granted — until it stops working. This guide covers everything you need to know about automatic gate systems in Singapore, from choosing the right gate type and motor, to understanding safety devices, power failure behaviour, maintenance, and common faults.

Key Takeaways
  • Auto gate motors for swing and sliding gates run on 230VAC mains power and are not battery-backed. On power failure, the gate remains closed — perimeter security is preserved.
  • Motor selection should be based on gate weight, gate width, and daily cycle count — not price alone. An undersized motor is the single most common cause of premature failure.
  • Where a swing gate leaf exceeds 2 metres in width, a swing arm motor is recommended over a concealed motor — wider gates require more torque and greater resistance to wind loading.
  • Safety beams are not optional. Built-in obstacle detection only responds after the gate has made contact with an obstruction. A safety beam prevents contact from occurring in the first place.
  • Gate system service life in Singapore is typically 5 to 10 years — but this depends on the condition of the entire mechanical system, not just the motor. Track cleanliness, lubrication, hinge condition, and gate alignment all affect how long the motor lasts.
  • An electrical isolator must be installed near the gate motor on every installation, regardless of property type.
  • Car park barriers use boom arms rated for high-cycle vehicle throughput. They do not restrict pedestrian access — where pedestrian control is also required, a gate or separate access point is needed.
Understanding the System

1. What Is an Auto Gate System?

An automatic gate system allows residents and property owners to open and close a gate without leaving their vehicle. The gate is driven by an electric motor and can be controlled using a remote control, access card, smartphone application, intercom system, or licence plate recognition camera.

In Singapore, auto gate systems are found almost exclusively on landed properties — terrace houses, semi-detached homes, bungalows, and gated private estates. For condominiums and commercial properties, the equivalent system is typically a car park barrier, which is covered separately in Section 5.

Most people think of an auto gate as a convenience feature. In practice, it also forms an important part of a property's security and access management strategy. A properly designed gate system performs three functions: it controls who and what enters the property; it signals to the outside world that the premises are managed and monitored; and when integrated with an intercom or access reader, it becomes a fully automated entry management point.

Key Components of an Auto Gate System

1

The Gate Motor

The motor generates the torque required to move the gate. It is the most critical component in the system and the one most likely to be underspecified. The motor must be matched to the gate weight, gate width, daily cycle count, and site conditions. A correctly selected motor operates more smoothly, experiences less mechanical stress, and generally provides a longer and more reliable service life.

2

The Control Board

The electronic brain of the system. It receives signals from remote controls, access readers, intercom systems, and safety devices, and determines when the motor should open, stop, slow down, or reverse. The control board also manages opening and closing speed, automatic closing timers, safety logic, and obstacle detection responses.

3

Safety Devices

Safety beams and safety edges help prevent injury and damage. These devices monitor the gate opening and stop or reverse gate movement when an obstruction is detected. A gate system without proper safety devices should never be considered complete — they are engineering requirements, not optional accessories.

4

The Remote Control

The remote control is usually what homeowners interact with most frequently. Modern systems use encrypted rolling-code technology — a system where a new encrypted signal is generated and transmitted each time the remote is pressed. Because the code changes with every use, it cannot be copied and replayed by an unauthorised device. This provides significantly better security than older fixed-code systems, where the transmitted signal remains the same every time and can be captured and replayed using readily available equipment.

5

The Entry Device

The gate motor controls movement. Something else must decide who is permitted to trigger it. Depending on the installation, this function is performed by a standalone card or fob reader mounted at the gate post, an audio or video intercom that allows visitors to call the homeowner who can then release the gate remotely, or an integrated intercom with a built-in access reader combining visitor communication with credential-based access in a single unit. The entry device is not strictly part of the gate motor system, but it is closely connected to it. Integration options are covered in Section 4.

Understanding Your Options

2. Swing Gates vs Sliding Gates

The two most common gate types used on Singapore landed properties are swing gates and sliding gates. The correct choice depends primarily on the layout of the property rather than personal preference.

Swing Gates

A swing gate operates like a door. The gate leaf rotates around hinges mounted to a gate post and swings inward into the property. For most landed homes in Singapore, swing gates remain the most common choice. They offer a clean appearance, simple operation, and require no ground track.

The main considerations are driveway depth — there must be enough clearance for the gate swing arc — and gradient, as swing gates are not well suited to steeply sloped driveways.

Practical Rule of Thumb

Where a gate leaf exceeds approximately 2 metres in width, a swing arm motor is generally recommended over a concealed motor. A wider gate leaf is heavier and requires more torque to open and close reliably. Wind loading is also a significant factor — a wide gate presents a large surface area, and the mechanical arm of a swing arm motor provides better bracing against wind pressure than a concealed mechanism. For gate leaves within 2 metres, a concealed motor remains a practical and aesthetically clean choice for most residential installations.

Sliding Gates

A sliding gate moves sideways along a ground track, travelling parallel to the boundary wall rather than swinging inward. Sliding gates are commonly chosen when driveway space is limited, when the gate is particularly large or heavy, or when the driveway is on a slope.

The key considerations are that a ground track must be installed and maintained — dirt, stones, and debris in the track increase resistance and accelerate motor wear — and wall space beside the opening must be available to accommodate the gate travel.

Cantilever Sliding Gates

For premium installations or sites where a ground track is not practical, a cantilever sliding gate is an alternative. The gate is suspended from an upper rail rather than running on a ground track, eliminating the track channel at ground level entirely. This is sometimes specified for architecturally sensitive properties or where ground conditions make track installation difficult.

Situation Recommended Option
Standard landed property, leaf under 2m wide Swing gate — concealed motor
Swing gate leaf wider than 2m Swing gate — arm motor
Limited driveway depth Sliding gate
Heavy steel gate Sliding gate
Sloping driveway Sliding gate
Premium finish, no ground track preferred Cantilever sliding gate
Choosing the Right Motor

3. Motor Selection

Selecting the correct motor is one of the most important decisions in any gate installation. Motor selection should never be based on price alone — it should be based on gate type, gate weight, gate width, expected daily cycle count, and site conditions. An undersized motor operates under constant stress, wears faster, and typically fails earlier than a correctly specified one.

Concealed Swing Motors

Concealed motors are installed within the gate post or below the gate structure, hiding the mechanism from view entirely. Because no mechanical components are visible on the exterior, they provide the cleanest possible appearance and are often preferred for premium residential properties.

The trade-off is higher installation cost, drainage requirements that must be carefully managed to prevent water accumulation in the motor housing, and more complex access for servicing. Concealed motors are best suited to gate leaves up to 2 metres in width.

Swing Arm Motors

Swing arm motors use a visible mechanical arm connected to the gate leaf. Although the arm remains visible, these systems are robust, easier to maintain, and suitable for a wider range of gate sizes and weights. They are the correct choice when the gate leaf exceeds 2 metres in width, when the gate is particularly heavy, or when wind loading on a wide gate leaf is a consideration. Swing arm motors remain one of the most common choices for both residential and commercial swing gate installations.

Sliding Gate Motors

Sliding gate motors drive a gear rack — a toothed metal strip fixed along the base of the gate that meshes with a drive gear on the motor. As the motor turns, the drive gear travels along the gear rack, pushing the gate along its track. These motors are designed to handle heavier gates and higher operating cycles and are commonly used on industrial properties, commercial buildings, large residential gates, and logistics facilities.

Why Gate Weight Matters

Many homeowners focus on gate width when specifying a motor. In practice, gate weight is often the more important factor. Two gates may have identical dimensions but very different weights depending on the materials used. An aluminium gate is relatively light. A steel gate of the same dimensions can be significantly heavier. A motor that is correctly specified for one may be entirely unsuitable for the other.

When a motor is undersized, it works harder with every cycle, components wear faster, reliability decreases, and service life is shortened.

Duty Cycle Ratings

Gate motors are rated for a number of operating cycles per day. Residential motors are typically rated for light to medium duty — suitable for the cycle counts of a single-family home. Where usage is higher, such as a property with multiple vehicles or a commercial entrance, a motor rated for higher daily cycles should be selected. Specifying a residential-rated motor on a high-cycle application is a common cause of early failure.

Securevision Recommendation

As a general principle, we recommend selecting a motor with capacity above the gate's actual weight and expected cycle count. This provides operating headroom and helps compensate for Singapore's humidity, mechanical wear, ageing hinges, and the increased friction that develops in any mechanical system over time. A motor running comfortably within its design limits will almost always outlast one operating at or near maximum capacity from day one.

Smarter Entry Management

4. Access Control Integration

An auto gate becomes significantly more capable when integrated with an access control system. Rather than functioning purely as a motorised gate, it becomes part of a broader entry management strategy. Modern gate systems can be integrated with remote controls, access cards, key fobs, mobile applications, intercom systems, QR code readers, loop detectors, and licence plate recognition cameras.

Smartphone Control

Many modern gate systems can be controlled directly from a smartphone via Wi-Fi or a cloud-based application. This allows homeowners to open the gate remotely, admit deliveries, grant contractor access, and monitor gate activity. It is particularly useful for homeowners who travel frequently or spend significant time away from the property.

Card and Fob Access

RFID cards and key fobs provide a convenient alternative to traditional remote controls and are commonly used where multiple users require access, temporary access needs to be granted, or access rights need to be managed centrally. Access can also be restricted by time period if required — for example, limiting a helper's gate access to working hours only.

QR Code Access

Where the gate is integrated with an intercom or access reader that supports QR code functionality, temporary QR codes can be issued to visitors, delivery personnel, and contractors for controlled single-use or time-limited access. This eliminates the need to issue physical credentials for short-term visitors.

Loop Detectors

A loop detector is a sensor embedded in the ground that detects the presence of a vehicle by sensing changes in the electromagnetic field caused by the vehicle's metal mass. In landed property installations, loop detectors are not commonly used at the entrance but are sometimes installed on long driveways to detect a departing vehicle and trigger the gate to open automatically for exit — eliminating the need for the driver to use a remote on the way out.

In condominiums and apartment developments, loop detectors are quite commonly used in conjunction with both swing and sliding gates, primarily for egress. A vehicle approaching the exit lane triggers the loop detector, which signals the gate to open automatically without the driver needing to present a credential.

Licence Plate Recognition

Licence plate recognition (LPR) systems identify authorised vehicles by their registration plate. When a recognised vehicle approaches, the camera captures the plate, the software verifies it against an approved list, and the gate opens automatically. This technology is increasingly used in condominiums, private estates, and commercial properties where seamless vehicle access is a priority. For more detail on LPR integration, see our Vehicle & LPR Management systems page.

Managing Vehicle Access at Scale

5. Car Park Barriers

Car park barriers are designed for a different purpose from gate systems. While an auto gate secures an entire entrance opening, a barrier focuses on controlling vehicle throughput at high volume.

The typical residential gate may open and close 10 to 20 times in a day. A condominium car park barrier may cycle hundreds of times daily. The mechanical design, motor specifications, and duty cycle requirements are therefore fundamentally different.

The standard barrier type used in Singapore condominiums and commercial car parks is the boom barrier — a counterbalanced arm that raises and lowers to permit vehicle entry or exit. A boom barrier controls vehicles but does not prevent pedestrians from walking around or underneath the arm. Where pedestrian control is also required, a gate or separate pedestrian access point is needed.

Loop detectors are standard in most condominium barrier installations, used to detect vehicle presence at the exit lane and trigger automatic arm raising for egress.

Protecting People and Property

6. Safety Devices

Safety devices are among the most important components in any automatic gate system — and among the most frequently overlooked. A moving gate can weigh several hundred kilograms. A sliding gate or swing gate moving at full speed can cause serious injury or damage if it strikes a person, child, pet, or vehicle. For this reason, safety devices should never be treated as optional accessories.

Safety Beams

A safety beam consists of a transmitter and a receiver mounted on opposite sides of the gate opening. An invisible infrared beam is projected across the opening. If a person, vehicle, child, bicycle, or pet interrupts the beam while the gate is closing, the control board stops or reverses the gate immediately.

Without a safety beam, the gate may only react after making physical contact with an obstacle through its built-in force detection. A safety beam prevents contact from occurring in the first place and should be considered essential on every automatic gate installation.

Safety Edges

A safety edge is a pressure-sensitive strip mounted to the leading edge of the gate leaf. If the gate makes contact with any object, the strip sends an immediate stop signal to the control board. Safety edges are particularly important on sliding gates and are also recommended on swing gate installations in high-traffic environments.

Obstacle Detection

Most modern gate motors include built-in obstacle detection. The motor continuously monitors resistance during operation and will stop or reverse if excessive resistance is detected. This is a useful secondary layer of protection but should not replace dedicated safety beams and safety edges.

Securevision Position

Safety beams and safety edges are standard requirements on every system we install — not optional additions. If you are comparing quotations and a competitor's price does not include safety devices, the scope is not equivalent. Ask them to add it and compare again.

A Question We Hear Every Day

7. Power Failure Behaviour

One of the most common questions homeowners ask is what happens to the gate during a power failure. The answer is different from what many people expect.

Unlike side gate electric locks — which typically operate on 12VDC and can be backed by a battery system — auto gate motors for swing and sliding gates run on 230VAC mains power. They are not normally backed by battery systems.

When mains power is lost, the gate motor stops. The gate remains in its current position. In most cases, if the gate was closed when the power failed, it will remain closed. A power failure does not compromise perimeter security for the gate itself. The gate stays shut.

Operating the Gate Manually During a Power Failure

Because the motor is not running, the gate must be released mechanically before it can be moved by hand.

Swing gates use a centre lock with a solenoid-operated latch. During a power failure, a bypass key is required to release the solenoid and unlock the centre lock, allowing the gate to be pushed open manually.

Sliding gates use a gear rack driven by the motor. A bypass key is required to disengage the gear rack from the motor drive, after which the gate can be pushed along its track manually.

Site Experience

We regularly receive calls from homeowners who have been unable to exit during a power failure because they could not locate the bypass key. The key is typically stored in a small cover or compartment on or near the motor housing. Find it now — before you need it in the dark.

Car Park Barriers During a Power Failure

Car park barriers also run on mains power. On power failure, the barrier arm will remain in its last position — typically lowered (closed). Most barriers include a manual crank or lever mechanism that allows the arm to be raised by hand to permit vehicle exit.

For applications where the barrier must automatically raise on power failure — such as certain managed estates or critical exit points — an optional capacitor can be fitted. This capacitor releases stored energy on power failure to automatically raise the arm, ensuring vehicles are not trapped. This is an optional accessory, not standard on most residential or commercial installations.

For truly critical infrastructure applications, a UPS may be used to maintain powered operation of barriers and gates during an outage. In our experience, this is not commonly required for landed properties or condominiums.

Getting the Specification Right

8. Site Assessment & Planning

Every successful gate installation begins with a proper site assessment. The correct motor and system configuration cannot be determined from photographs or a brief telephone conversation alone.

Factors that must be evaluated on-site include gate width, gate weight, ground conditions, drainage, structural condition of gate posts and pillars, power supply routing, and the location of the electrical isolator.

Why Drainage Matters

This is particularly relevant for concealed motor installations. Concealed motors are installed within the gate post structure and rely on proper drainage to prevent water accumulation. Poor drainage leads to corrosion, electrical faults, and premature motor failure. Drainage should be treated as part of the installation scope, not an afterthought.

Why Gate Posts Matter

The gate motor transfers all its operating force into the gate posts. Posts that are weak, poorly constructed, or beginning to shift can crack, move, or cause the gate to fall out of alignment. This affects motor performance and accelerates wear. A proper site assessment identifies structural issues before the installation begins rather than during it.

Electrical Isolator Requirement

An electrical isolator must be installed near the gate motor for every installation — whether on a landed property, condominium, commercial building, or industrial site. The isolator allows the power supply to the motor to be safely disconnected for troubleshooting, servicing, or motor replacement without requiring access to the main distribution board. This is a standard requirement on every properly installed system.

Electrical Supply Routing

The distance between the electrical distribution board and the gate motor location affects both installation cost and complexity. On sites where the gate is far from the nearest power source, cable routing — whether surface-mounted, buried in conduit, or run through existing ducting — needs to be planned as part of the site assessment. This is a factor that can significantly affect the scope and cost of civil works required.

Understanding What It Costs

9. Investment Guide

The cost of an automatic gate system depends on gate type, motor selection, civil works required, electrical works, safety devices, and access control integration. Because every site is different, a site assessment is usually required before a final recommendation and quotation can be prepared.

Why the Cheapest Quote Is Not Always the Lowest Cost

When comparing quotations, it is important to understand what is actually included. Differences between quotations often come down to motor quality, inclusion of safety devices, installation quality and workmanship, warranty coverage, and after-sales support availability. A lower upfront price may result in higher maintenance and replacement costs over the life of the system.

Questions to Ask Before Accepting a Quote

Confirm whether the quotation includes: safety beams and safety edges, cabling and conduit, civil works (track, ducting, concrete where required), an electrical isolator, commissioning and testing, user training, warranty period, and after-sales support. Two quotations that appear similar in price may include very different scopes of work.

Quote Transparency

Be cautious of quotations that list only the motor price. A quote that omits safety devices, cabling, civil preparation, or the electrical isolator will be cheaper on paper — but not in practice. These items will be added as variations during installation.

Keeping Your System Running

11. Maintenance & Servicing

Singapore's climate is demanding for gate systems. Heat, humidity, and frequent rainfall place continuous stress on mechanical and electrical components. Regular maintenance is the single most effective way to extend system life and prevent unexpected failures.

How Long Should a Gate Motor Last?

A well-maintained gate system in regular residential use can be expected to provide 5 to 10 years of reliable service. However, the motor is only one part of the equation.

The overall service life depends heavily on the condition of the entire mechanical system — not just the motor itself. Key factors include:

  • Track cleanliness — dirt, stones, and mud in the ground track increase resistance and place additional load on the motor
  • Lubrication — wheels, gears, rollers (for sliding gates), and hinges must be kept properly lubricated using an appropriate product such as a silicone-based or lithium-based grease. General-purpose oils and spray lubricants such as WD-40 are not suitable — they attract dust and grit and can accelerate wear over time
  • Gate balance and alignment — a gate that is sagging, warped, or misaligned causes friction that puts continuous stress on the motor
  • Hinge condition — worn or poorly maintained hinges on swing gates cause the gate to drop and drag, increasing the load on the motor with every cycle

Friction and misalignment are among the most common causes of shortened motor life. A motor that is fighting a poorly maintained gate structure will fail earlier than one driving a well-maintained gate — regardless of its original quality.

Gear Slippage

In some older or poorly maintained systems, wear on the gear rack or motor drive gear can cause gear slippage — where the gate fails to complete its full travel or drifts back after reaching its position. This is not always immediately obvious and can be mistaken for a motor or control board fault. Regular inspection of the gear rack and drive gear condition is part of a thorough annual service.

Annual Service Checklist

A service visit should typically include: lubrication of all moving parts with appropriate products; cleaning of the ground track and removal of debris; inspection and adjustment of gate alignment and balance; tightening of all electrical connections; testing of safety beams and safety edges; operational testing across the full range of motion; visual inspection of hinges, rollers, brackets, and hardware; and battery check for any ancillary electric lock systems.

Between Service Visits

Homeowners can perform simple checks between service visits: keep safety beam lenses clean and free of dust, cobwebs, and moisture; listen for unusual sounds — grinding, squeaking, or knocking that were not present before; observe gate movement for hesitation, scraping, or incomplete travel; check that the remote operates from a normal distance; and inspect visible hardware for rust, looseness, or misalignment. Problems identified early are almost always easier and less expensive to resolve than those left to develop into full failures.

When Something Goes Wrong

12. Common Faults & Troubleshooting

Most gate faults have relatively straightforward causes. Understanding the symptoms helps homeowners decide whether the issue is minor or whether professional attention is required.

Securevision Verdict

The most important decisions in any gate installation are not about brand. They are about whether the motor is correctly sized for the gate, whether proper safety devices are included, whether the site has been properly assessed, and whether the installer will still be available to support the system long after the installation is complete. A properly specified, correctly installed, and well-maintained gate system can provide a decade or more of reliable service. A poorly specified system — one where the motor is undersized, safety devices are omitted, or the gate structure is ignored — will become a source of ongoing frustration and expense regardless of which brand of motor was used. Take the time to assess the site properly, select the right motor for the gate and the application, and prioritise safety. Doing so will almost always deliver better long-term value than choosing the lowest quotation.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a swing gate and a sliding gate?

A swing gate opens like a door and requires space within the property for the gate leaves to swing inward. A sliding gate moves sideways along a ground track and is preferred where driveway depth is limited or the gate is particularly heavy.

Can I automate my existing gate?

In many cases, yes. If the gate structure remains sound, a motor can often be added without replacing the gate itself. A site assessment will confirm suitability.

How do I choose the right gate motor?

Motor selection should be based on gate type, gate weight, gate width, expected daily cycle count, and site conditions — not price alone.

How long does a gate motor last?

A well-maintained system in regular residential use can typically provide 5 to 10 years of service. The condition of the entire mechanical system — track, hinges, rollers, and gate balance — has as much influence on service life as the motor itself.

Does my swing gate need a safety beam?

Yes. We strongly recommend safety beams on all automatic gate installations. A safety beam detects obstructions before the gate makes contact. Built-in obstacle detection alone only responds after contact has occurred.

What happens to my gate during a power failure?

Auto gate motors run on 230VAC mains power and are not battery-backed. On power failure, the gate remains in its current position — if it was closed, it stays closed, preserving perimeter security. A bypass key is required to release the gate mechanically for manual operation.

What happens to a car park barrier during a power failure?

The barrier arm remains in its last position. Most barriers include a manual crank to raise the arm by hand. An optional capacitor can be fitted to automatically raise the arm on power failure — this is typically specified for managed developments with specific egress requirements.

Can I open my gate using my mobile phone?

Many modern gate systems support smartphone control through Wi-Fi or cloud-based applications.

Can my gate work with an intercom system?

Yes. Modern intercom systems can be integrated with gate motors, allowing homeowners to communicate with visitors and release the gate remotely.

Can licence plate recognition open my gate automatically?

Yes. LPR systems can recognise approved vehicles and trigger the gate to open automatically on approach.

Can visitors access the gate using a QR code?

Where the gate is integrated with an intercom or access reader that supports QR code functionality, temporary QR codes can be issued to visitors and contractors for time-limited or single-use access.

Why does my gate not close properly at the centre?

Common causes include gate sagging, worn hinges, misaligned brackets, structural movement, and incorrect motor limit settings. In most cases the motor is functioning correctly and the issue lies with the gate structure.

Why does my gate reverse when closing?

Usually a safety response — the system has detected excessive resistance or a safety beam obstruction. Check the safety beam alignment before assuming a motor fault.

Why is my remote control not working?

The most common causes are a flat battery, lost programming, or a receiver fault. Try a second remote and check the motor power supply at the isolator first.

How often should my gate be serviced?

Residential gate systems should be serviced annually.

How long does installation take?

Most residential installations can be completed within one to two days, depending on the scope of civil and electrical works.

How do I choose the right installer?

Look beyond the motor brand. Consider the installer's experience, whether proper safety devices are included in the scope, quality of workmanship, spare parts availability, and whether the company will still be available to support the system in five years' time.
