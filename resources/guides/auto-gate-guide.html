Foundation of Access Control

1. What Is an Auto Gate System?

An automatic gate system is a motorised assembly that opens and closes a gate using an electric motor, triggered by a remote control, access reader, or sensor.

More Than Just Convenience

A gate motor that works quietly and reliably every time is easy to take for granted — until it fails. This guide is written for the moment you need to make a real decision: choosing a system for a new home, replacing a failed motor, or upgrading to one with access control integration.

An auto gate system has three core jobs. First, it controls who and what enters your property. Second, it signals to the outside world that the premises are managed and monitored. Third, when integrated with an intercom or access reader, it becomes a fully automated entry management point.

Key Components of the System

1

The Gate Motor

The motor generates the torque required to push or pull the gate leaf. Selecting an underpowered motor for your gate weight is the single most common cause of premature failure. The motor must be matched to the gate's weight, width, and daily cycle count.

2

The Control Board

The electronic brain of the system. It receives signals from remotes, access readers, and safety sensors, and instructs the motor to open, stop, or reverse. The control board also manages timing, speed ramping, and obstacle detection logic.

3

Safety Devices

Photocell safety beams and safety edges are mandatory on any responsibly installed gate. They detect obstructions in the gate's path and signal the control board to halt or reverse immediately. No safety device means no safe gate.

4

Remote Keyfob

The remote keyfob is what most homeowners interact with every day. It transmits a radio frequency (RF) signal — typically at 433MHz or 868MHz — to a receiver connected to the control board. Effective range is generally 5–10 metres in open air, though walls, vehicles, and RF interference from neighbouring remotes can reduce this in practice.

Not all remotes are equal. Basic remotes use a fixed code — the same signal is transmitted every time the button is pressed. Fixed-code remotes are vulnerable to code grabbing, where a device records the signal and replays it to open your gate without a physical remote. Rolling-code (hopping-code) remotes solve this by generating a new encrypted code with every button press. The receiver and remote are synchronised so only the next expected code in the sequence is accepted. Even if someone captures the transmission, it is already invalid by the time they attempt to use it. For any gate installation, rolling-code remotes are the correct choice.

Understanding Your Options

2. Gate Types: Swing vs Sliding

The two dominant gate types in Singapore — swing and sliding — suit different property configurations and space requirements. The right choice is determined by your driveway geometry, not by preference alone.

Swing Gates

A swing gate operates like a door, rotating around a vertical hinge post. As a general rule, gates should swing inward into the premises. Outward swinging is only acceptable when there is no pedestrian footpath risk — which in most Singapore landed properties means it is rarely the correct choice. Dual-leaf swing gates (two leaves meeting in the centre) are the most common configuration for Singapore terrace and semi-detached properties.

Sliding Gates

A sliding gate travels horizontally along a ground track parallel to the boundary wall. It is the preferred solution for properties with limited driveway depth — where a swing arc would block the vehicle before it clears the road. Sliding gates are also the correct choice for heavier gates and for properties with sloped driveways where a swing gate would drag on the ground.

Feature Swing Gate Sliding Gate
Space requirement Swing arc clearance inside the property Wall run beside the opening (1× gate width)
Best suited for Most Singapore landed homes with standard driveways Limited driveway depth, sloped ground, heavy gates
Maximum gate weight Up to ~400kg per leaf (FAAC 415) Up to 1,500kg (FAAC 844)
Ground track required No Yes — embedded or surface-mounted
Cycle durability Standard residential duty Higher — preferred for high-cycle commercial use
Choosing the Right Motor

3. Motor Selection Guide

Motor selection is determined by four factors: gate type (swing or sliding), gate leaf width, gate weight, and expected daily cycle count. Getting this wrong is the most expensive mistake in a gate installation — an undersized motor fails prematurely and a replacement mid-cycle becomes a costly emergency.

Concealed Swing Motors

For leaves up to 2m wide with standard residential weight, a concealed motor — such as the MAG SW200P — is buried within the gate post or ground housing, leaving no visible mechanical components on the exterior. This is the cleanest aesthetic option and the most popular choice for Singapore landed homes where visual presentation matters. The motor and all cabling are completely hidden from view.

Swing Arm Motors

When the gate leaf exceeds 2m or the gate is particularly heavy, a swing arm motor — such as the FAAC 415 — is the correct choice. The motor arm is visible above the gate hinge but the mechanism is robust and long-lived. This is the standard specification for condominium main entrance gates and for commercial properties with oversize gate leaves. The FAAC 415 is one of the most widely deployed gate motors in Singapore.

Sliding Gate Motors

We carry two FAAC sliding motor models. The FAAC 746 handles gates up to 600kg and is specified for residential and light commercial sliding gates. The FAAC 844 handles gates up to 1,500kg and is the correct choice for industrial premises, logistics hubs, and any application where a heavy steel gate requires a high-cycle motor that will not fail under daily commercial use.

Securevision Recommendation

Always size the motor to at least 20% above your gate's measured weight. Singapore's tropical humidity accelerates wear on gate hinges and tracks — a motor operating at its rated limit from day one will reach the end of its service life significantly earlier than one with headroom to spare.

Smarter Entry Management

4. Access Control Integration

A gate motor on its own is an automation device. A gate motor integrated with an access control system is an entry management point. The difference matters for security, audit trails, and convenience.

Smartphone and Wi-Fi Control

Wi-Fi modules can be added to most existing gate motors, allowing operation from a smartphone from anywhere in the world. This is particularly useful for admitting deliveries or contractors when you are not at home. The module connects to your home Wi-Fi and pairs with a mobile app. More sophisticated setups integrate the gate trigger with the intercom system — a visitor presses the doorbell, you see them on your phone, and tap to open the gate.

Card and Fob Access

An RFID reader mounted beside the gate allows registered cards or key fobs to trigger the gate without a remote. This is the standard solution for households with domestic helpers, regular deliveries, or properties where multiple people need gate access without being issued remotes. Access can be time-restricted — a helper's card may only work during working hours.

Licence Plate Recognition (LPR)

Increasingly viable for condominium developments and landed estates where authorised vehicles are identified by their registration plate and the gate opens automatically on recognition. LPR systems require a camera positioned to capture the plate clearly, processing software to match against an approved list, and a relay trigger connected to the gate motor's control board. Securevision integrates LPR with the FAAC gate motors we install for condominiums and larger residential developments.

Managing Vehicle Access at Scale

5. Car Park Barriers

Car park barriers are designed for throughput, not perimeter closure. Where a residential gate may open and close 20–30 times per day, a condominium car park barrier may cycle 300–500 times. The mechanism is fundamentally different — a counterbalanced boom arm rather than a full gate leaf — and the motor is rated for high-cycle commercial duty accordingly.

We carry the NICE range of Italian-manufactured barriers, which are specified for high-cycle environments including condominium car parks, commercial buildings, and industrial logistics hubs. NICE barriers integrate with access control systems, LPR cameras, and intercom panels using standard dry contact relay triggers.

Barrier vs Gate — When to Use Which

Use a gate when you need to control pedestrian access as well as vehicles — the gate closes the full opening. Use a barrier when vehicle throughput is the primary requirement and pedestrian access is managed separately through a turnstile or staffed guardhouse. A barrier arm that is raised does not prevent pedestrians from walking under or around it.

Protecting People and Property

6. Safety Devices

Safety devices are not optional extras. They are engineering requirements for any responsibly installed gate. A moving gate leaf that weighs 150–400kg and strikes a person or vehicle causes serious injury and significant liability. The safety devices below are standard on every Securevision installation.

Photocell Safety Beams

A transmitter and receiver are mounted on opposite sides of the gate opening, creating an invisible beam at vehicle bumper height. When the beam is interrupted — by a vehicle, person, or object — it signals the control board to halt or reverse the gate immediately. Photocells must be tested during every service visit. Common failure modes include misalignment caused by post movement and blocked lenses from insects or spider webs.

Safety Edges

A rubber-encased pressure-sensitive strip mounted on the leading edge of the gate leaf. On contact with any obstruction, it cuts the motor signal immediately. Safety edges are mandatory for sliding gates and strongly recommended for swing gates on busy driveways.

Securevision Requirement

A gate motor without safety devices is a liability. Photocells and safety edges are standard engineering requirements on every system we install. If you are obtaining competitive quotes and a competitor's price does not include safety devices, the scope is not equivalent — ask them to add it and compare again.

Getting the Specification Right

7. Site Assessment & Planning

Every gate installation begins with a site assessment. Gate width, ground conditions, wall structure, power supply routing, and drainage all determine which system is viable. Skipping this step — accepting a quote based on photographs alone — is how homeowners end up with the wrong motor or a system that requires expensive remedial civil work.

Assessment Point What We Check Why It Matters
Gate opening width Clear measurement between posts Minimum 3m clear opening for single-lane vehicle access
Pillar and post condition Material, reinforcement, and crack inspection Brick pillars without steel reinforcement crack under motor forces over time
Gate weight and leaf width Measured or estimated from material Determines motor model and power requirements
Power supply routing Distance from nearest DB, conduit options Determines cable size and whether a dedicated circuit is required
Ground conditions Surface type, drainage, slope Affects track embedding for sliding gates and photocell mounting positions

Keeping Your System Running

10. Maintenance & Servicing

Gate motors in Singapore's tropical climate are subject to heat, humidity, and rain exposure that accelerates wear on mechanical and electrical components. Annual servicing is not a recommendation — it is the difference between a 10-year motor and a 5-year motor.

Annual Servicing Schedule

A Securevision annual service covers: lubrication of all moving parts (gear rack, hinges, pivot points), inspection and tightening of all electrical connections, battery backup load test (replace if below 80% capacity), photocell beam alignment and function test, full open/close cycle test with obstruction simulation, and visual inspection of gate post condition and gate leaf fasteners.

Between Service Visits

Homeowners should check monthly: that photocell lenses are clear of webs and debris, that the gate travels smoothly without grinding or hesitation, that the battery backup indicator on the control board shows healthy charge, and that remotes respond on the first button press without repeated attempts.

When Something Goes Wrong

11. Common Faults & Troubleshooting

Most gate faults have simple causes. Before calling for a service visit, work through these checks:

Fault Most Common Cause First Check
Gate reverses immediately on opening Safety device triggered Check photocell lenses for webs, leaves, or misalignment. Clear and retry
Remote not working Battery or signal issue Try the second remote. If neither works, check the motor's circuit breaker and isolator switch
Gate moves slowly or hesitates Lubrication or motor wear Check gear rack for dry spots. If lubrication does not resolve, call for service
Gate does not open during power failure Battery backup depleted Check battery indicator on control board. Battery replacement is typically needed every 3–5 years
Gate stops mid-travel Obstacle, limit switch, or motor overload Clear any visible obstruction. If it repeats, the travel limits may need recalibration — call for service
Loud grinding noise on operation Dry gear rack or worn pinion Lubricate the rack immediately. Continued dry running causes rapid wear to both rack and motor pinion
