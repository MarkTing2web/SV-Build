Foundation of Protection

1. What Is a Burglar Alarm System?

A burglar alarm system is a critical network of sensors and intelligence designed to detect unauthorised breach and trigger immediate deterrent responses. In the Singapore context, it serves as the primary psychological and physical line of defence for properties ranging from residential landed homes to high-security commercial vaults.

Evolution of Modern Security

Burglars do not just steal material possessions — they steal your peace of mind. Studies consistently show that homes without burglar alarms are significantly more likely to be broken into than homes with visible alarm systems. The presence of alarm signage alone acts as a powerful deterrent, signalling to would-be intruders that your property is protected.

Beyond deterrence, modern burglar alarms provide real-time alerts directly to your mobile phone, allowing you to respond immediately whether you are at home, at work, or travelling overseas. Integration with CCTV systems enables video verification — you can see what triggered the alarm and make informed decisions about whether to call the police.

Critical Strategic Capabilities

Today's burglar alarms go far beyond simple detection, serving as a unified security hub:

  • Mobile Ecosystem: Receive instant push notifications and arm or disarm your property from anywhere via encrypted cloud apps.
  • Visual Verification: Seamless integration with IP cameras allows for rapid verification of triggers, eliminating false alarm fatigue.
  • Adaptive Arming: Distinct profiles for Away, Stay, and Night modes ensure appropriate protection levels at all times.
  • Building Automation: Link alarms to smart lighting and access control systems for a complete, reactive security ecosystem.
System Architecture

2. How Does a Burglar Alarm System Work?

An intrusion system relies on the seamless interplay between hardware detection and software logic. Understanding these core components is essential for designing a resilient security envelope.

The Six Core Components

1

Control Panel — The Processor

The control panel serves as the central intelligent hub. Installed in a secure internal location, it continuously monitors every sensor while managing user authentication and emergency communication protocols. Modern panels feature multi-zone processing — precise identification of which area triggered an alarm. High-end units like RISCO or Paradox can handle 50+ individual zones with independent arming logic.

2

User Interfaces — Keypads

Your primary point of interaction. Keypads allow arming and disarming, status checks, and viewing alarm history. Modern options range from traditional tactile buttons to high-fidelity LCD touchscreens with intuitive, app-like navigation. Positioned at entry and exit points and in the master bedroom.

3

Detection Devices — Sensors

Sensors are the eyes and ears of the system. They detect physical changes — a door opening, a heat-signature in motion — and instantly report the violation to the control panel. Sensor choice depends on the location, exposure to weather, and the type of breach being detected.

4

Notification — Sirens & Strobes

When a breach occurs, sirens execute high-decibel auditory deterrence. External strobe units provide visual identification, helping emergency responders or security staff locate the specific property under threat. Internal sounders disorientate the intruder and force them to vacate immediately.

5

Communication Modules

This layer ensures the alert leaves your property. Using IP (Internet), GSM (Mobile Data), or traditional landlines, the panel communicates triggers directly to your phone or a 24/7 Professional Monitoring Station. Dual-path communication — IP primary, GSM backup — is the standard for any serious installation.

6

Backup Power Unit

Security systems must survive power failures. Every panel contains a rechargeable 12V backup battery that automatically takes over during blackouts, providing hours of continued protection. Battery health should be tested annually — a failed backup battery is the most common point of failure in an ageing alarm system.

Sequential Operational Flow

A resilient system follows a strict logical sequence from detection to deterrence:

  1. Detection: A sensor (PIR or magnetic contact) is triggered and instantly transmits an encrypted signal to its assigned zone on the control panel.
  2. Logic Processing: The panel cross-references the arming state. If the zone is active, the processor initiates the alarm response logic.
  3. Entry Delay Check: If triggered via a designated entry door, a countdown begins. Failure to input a valid PIN triggers full-scale activation. Instant zones trigger without delay.
  4. Primary Deterrence: Internal and external high-decibel sirens activate. Strobe units flash to provide visual identification of the breach.
  5. Cloud Communication: The panel pushes alerts through primary (IP) and secondary (GSM) data paths. Users receive push notifications via cloud apps.
System Options

3. Types of Burglar Alarm Systems: Wired vs Wireless

Choosing the right system architecture depends on your property's lifecycle — whether you are currently in a renovation phase or looking for a rapid, non-invasive deployment.

Wired Systems — The Gold Standard

Wired systems use physical cables to connect every sensor and keypad back to the main control panel. Cabling is concealed within ceilings, walls, or architectural conduits. If you are currently renovating or building a new property, a wired system is the superior choice. Since the walls are open, running cables is highly cost-effective and provides the highest possible reliability without any battery maintenance for sensors.

Feature Wired System Wireless System
Installation timingDuring renovation / pre-move-inPost-renovation / occupied
Average labour time12–24 hours4–6 hours
Signal integrityPhysical copper cableEncrypted radio (AES-128)
Sensor battery maintenanceNone — sensors are poweredReplace every 2–3 years per sensor
Hardware flexibilityFixed locationsRelocatable, highly scalable
Sensor hardware costLower (~40% less)Higher

Wireless Systems — Modern Precision

Wireless systems use high-grade, battery-powered sensors that communicate via encrypted radio frequencies. These systems eliminate the need for data cabling, allowing for a clean installation that protects architectural finishes. Installation is rapid — typically under 6 hours — and entirely non-invasive. This makes them perfect for contemporary apartments, conservation projects, or rental units where drilling is restricted.

Securevision Engineering Verdict

Choose wired if you are in the renovation phase — the upfront labour is offset by life-long reliability and zero sensor battery maintenance. Choose wireless if your property is finished — you will benefit from state-of-the-art AES-128 encryption with zero impact on your décor. Both deliver equivalent security outcomes when correctly specified and installed.

Detection Layer

4. Sensors & Detectors Explained

The efficacy of your alarm system is defined by its detection surface. We deploy a range of specialised sensors to ensure early warning across every architectural vulnerability in your property.

Detection Components

1

Magnetic Contacts

The primary line of defence. These dual-part reed switches monitor the state of doors and windows, triggering instantly upon physical opening. We specify Grade 2 high-power magnets to prevent false alarms caused by loose door frames or thermal expansion in Singapore's tropical climate — a specific challenge in properties with aluminium-framed sliding doors.

2

PIR Motion Sensors

Detect moving heat signatures. We utilise pet-immune digital PIRs that distinguish between human intruders and small animals up to 25kg. Modern PIRs feature advanced Digital Signal Processing (DSP) to filter out thermal turbulence from air-conditioning units or direct sunlight — both common false alarm sources in Singapore properties.

3

Vibration & Shock Sensors

Specifically designed for glass facades and wall-breach detection. They detect the specific high-frequency signature of glass breaking or physical impact before the intruder enters — providing an earlier warning than a magnetic contact, which only triggers once the door or window is actually opened.

4

Internal Sounders

While external sirens alert the neighbourhood, internal sounders emit piercing 110dB tones to disorientate the intruder and force them to vacate the premises immediately. Effective placement is typically in ceiling-mounted positions in hallways and stairwells for maximum disorientation effect.

Advanced Detection Technologies

For high-sensitivity environments and landed properties with significant outdoor space, we deploy specialised detection technologies:

  • Infrared Beams: Create invisible point-to-point boundaries across driveways or fence lines. They trigger before the intruder reaches the building — the earliest possible warning layer.
  • Dual-Tech Sensors: Combine PIR with microwave frequency to virtually eliminate false alarms in unstable environments — near air-conditioning units, in direct sunlight, or in areas with frequent insect activity.
  • Smoke & Heat Integration: The alarm panel can serve as a life-safety fire alert system when smoke and heat detectors are added as additional zones.
System Design

5. Strategic System Design

Effective security is not about indiscriminate coverage — it is about protecting the right assets in the most logical sequence. We call this the Layered Defence approach.

The Three Layers of Defence

We approach every property as a series of nested perimeters, each providing a distinct layer of protection:

  • Layer 1 — Perimeter: Securing doors, windows, and balconies with magnetic contacts and vibration shock sensors. This stops entry before it happens.
  • Layer 2 — Interior: Deploying PIR motion sensors in common hallways and stairway landings to track movement if a perimeter breach occurs.
  • Layer 3 — Specific Asset: Targeted sensors on safes, server racks, or master suite doors for high-value protection.

Property-Specific Planning

Offices & Retail Public entrances, inventory rooms, panic buttons for staff safety Wired RISCO or Paradox with automatic disarming via access control integration
Apartments & Condos Main entrance door and balcony sliding doors Wireless kit — non-invasive, ideal where management rules restrict drilling
Landed Estates Comprehensive external perimeter before building entry Wired backbone with infrared beam perimeter and outdoor motion detectors
High-Value Commercial Vault, server room, high-value inventory storage Wired addressable system with dual-tech sensors and 24/7 CAMS monitoring

Six Factors That Determine Your Investment

  • Property size and layout: Larger properties require more sensors and complex wiring. Multi-storey properties add installation scope due to cable runs between floors.
  • Number of entry points: Every door and accessible window adds to the hardware count. A landed home may have 20+ entry points compared to an apartment's 5.
  • Wired vs wireless: Wired systems have lower sensor costs but higher labour. Wireless is faster to install but hardware is premium-priced.
  • Monitoring requirements: Self-monitoring is free; central monitoring (CAMS) adds annual professional fees.
  • Integration scope: Connecting to CCTV, access control, or smart home systems adds to configuration cost.
  • Installation timing: Wired systems installed during renovation are significantly cheaper than retrofits into a completed property.
How to Optimise Your Investment

Install during renovation whenever possible — wired systems are significantly cheaper during this phase. Focus coverage on accessible entry points and use motion sensors as a backup interior layer rather than trying to sensor every room. Starting with essential zones and expanding later is more cost-effective than over-engineering the first installation.

10. Maintenance & Testing Protocols

An alarm system provides a false sense of security unless it is rigorously tested. In Singapore's humid climate, sensor calibration and battery health are the two primary failure points.

The Quarterly Walk-Test

Maintain peak performance by executing these steps every 90 days:

  1. CAMS Notification: Inform your monitoring station before initiating a live test to avoid accidental police dispatch.
  2. Walk-Test Sequence: Systematic triggers for every door, window, and motion zone. Confirm sirens activate and strobe units flash correctly.
  3. Zone Mapping: Confirm that "Front Door" on the keypad screen truly matches the physical door trigger. Label mismatches are a common maintenance finding.

Critical Maintenance Schedule

Frequency Task Who Performs
Monthly Signal test — trigger one zone to confirm communication to phone or CAMS station Homeowner / office manager
Quarterly Full walk-test of all zones. Check battery indicators on wireless sensors Homeowner / office manager
Annually Deep system audit — battery impedance test, firmware update, tamper check, full zone verification Securevision engineer

12. Common Implementation Mistakes

Failed security systems are rarely due to equipment failure — they are almost always caused by poor design or psychological shortcuts taken during procurement or daily operation.

Mistake What Goes Wrong The Correct Approach
Reactive procurement after a nearby break-in Rushed, sub-optimal installations under emotional pressure. 80% of residential alarm inquiries occur in the 7 days following a local incident. Proactive hardening before an incident. Plan during renovation, not after a scare.
Under-securing perimeter points Focusing on the front door while ignoring side gates or ground-floor balconies. Professional burglars always target the point of least resistance. Full perimeter assessment. Every accessible entry point gets a sensor.
Shared codes for all users No audit trail. If something goes wrong, you cannot determine who armed or disarmed the system and when. Individual user codes for every person with access — family, helpers, contractors.
Consumer-grade DIY kits for high-value properties Lack the signal encryption and tamper-resilience required for meaningful protection of commercial or high-value residential assets. Professional-grade systems with AES-128 encryption and tamper-protected hardware.
Regulations & Requirements

13. Insurance Benefits & Singapore Compliance

Insurance Discounts

Many Singapore insurers offer 10–15% premium discounts for professionally monitored alarm systems. Ensure you have your Installation Certificate ready for audits — issued by Securevision on completion of every installation. Without this certificate, insurance claims relating to break-in incidents may be contested.

Police Licensing (PSIA)

In Singapore, only Police-licensed contractors are legally allowed to install security systems. Securevision maintains full licensing (L/PS/000267/2023P) to ensure your system is compliant and qualifies for insurance claims. When obtaining competitive quotes, always verify the contractor's PSIA licence number. An installation by an unlicensed contractor may invalidate your insurance coverage.

Compliance Checklist

Verify before any installation: contractor holds a valid PSIA licence, an Installation Certificate will be issued on completion, the system model is recognised by your insurer (check with your broker), and 24/7 CAMS monitoring is confirmed as sufficient for your policy requirements if commercial insurance is involved.

15. Frequently Asked Questions

Question Answer
What is the typical lifespan of an alarm system? A professionally installed wired system has a core lifecycle of 10–12 years. Wireless sensors typically require battery replacement every 3–5 years. We recommend a core panel upgrade every decade to maintain compatibility with modern cloud services.
Can I use my alarm if the Wi-Fi is down? Yes. All Securevision systems utilise local communication between sensors and the panel, independent of Wi-Fi. For external alerting, we configure dual-path communication (IP + 4G/GSM) to ensure signals reach you even if your fibre line fails.
Is there a monthly subscription fee? For self-monitoring via basic apps, there are typically no ongoing fees. For premium cloud features or 24/7 professional CAMS monitoring, a nominal monthly or annual fee applies to cover the cellular data and monitoring centre overhead.
Can I keep my existing system and just add sensors? Possibly — depends on the panel model and available zone capacity. We assess existing systems and advise on expandability before recommending replacement. If your panel is end-of-life, replacement is often more cost-effective than expansion.
How do I stop false alarms? Most false alarms in Singapore are caused by PIR sensors triggered by air-conditioning airflow, direct sunlight through windows, or pets. Specify pet-immune PIRs, position sensors away from air-con vents, and use dual-tech sensors in problematic locations.
